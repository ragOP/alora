Your pelvic floor is a group of powerful muscles that sit at the base of your pelvis.


This acts like a hammock to support your bladder, bowel, and intestines.


These muscles control the opening and closing of your bladder, preventing leaks.

But if you’ve had an enlarged prostate or undergone prostate surgery, these muscles often take a hit.


The nerves and tissues connected to the pelvic floor get weakened and disrupted,


This makes it harder to control leaks.


That’s why strengthening your pelvic floor is the key to stopping bladder leaks at the source.

Why Strengthening Your Pelvic Floor Actually Works

Many men don’t realize that pelvic floor training is crucial for bladder control.


Here’s why it can help with your condition:


🔹 Prostate Enlargement (BPH) – When the prostate swells, it squeezes the urethra (the tube that carries urine out of your bladder).


This makes it harder to fully empty your bladder.


This pressure leads to overflow, sudden urges, and dribbling after urination.


Stronger pelvic floor muscles help you suppress those bladder spasms and regain control.

🔹 Post-Prostate Surgery – Removing the prostate often damages the internal urinary sphincter


This is the muslce in the pelvic floor which controls the flow of urine.


That leaves the external sphincter to do all the work.


Training it is the most effective way to rebuild strength and stop leaks from escaping.

🔹 Obesity & Chronic Strain – Extra weight puts constant pressure on your pelvic floor.


On top of that, straining from constipation, chronic coughing, or heavy lifting increases this stress.


Over time, this repeated pressure weakens the muscles, making bladder control harder.

At first, you might only notice occasional leaks or sudden urges

But as your pelvic floor gets weaker, leaks happen more often and become harder to control.


If your muscles can’t support your bladder, no amount of “holding it in” will stop leaks.


That’s why pads, medications, and surgery don’t fix the root cause—they only mask the problem.

Why Most Solutions Don’t Work

If you’ve ever looked for a solution, you’ve probably realized that most options either don’t work or come with major downsides.


❌ Surgery? Some doctors suggest bladder sling procedures or even artificial sphincters.


But surgery comes with major risks, especially as you get older.


Recovery can take months or even years, and if you have heart disease, diabetes, or mobility issues, you may not even qualify for the procedure.


And here’s the worst part...


...even after surgery, leaks can still return.

❌ Medications? Doctors often prescribe bladder medications to reduce urgency.


But they don’t strengthen the muscles that control leaks—they only mask the problem.


And for many men, the side effects are worse than the leaks themselves.


Dry mouth, dizziness, constipation—even memory problems.


Some of these drugs are linked to an increased risk of dementia.


And to make things worse, certain medications raise blood pressure, making them risky for many older men.

❌ Medical Devices? Clamps that squeeze your urethra shut and catheters that you have to insert daily.


Most of these feel medieval — uncomfortable, awkward.


Not something any guy wants to rely on long-term.


For men with arthritis or mobility issues, daily use is even harder.


Other devices need constant adjustments.


Some require awkward positioning, making them frustrating to use.


And if a device is too complicated or uncomfortable, most men stop using it before it has a chance to work.

With so many disappointing options, many men give up and assume they just have to live with it.


But they don’t.
